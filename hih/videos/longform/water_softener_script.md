## 0. Hook (0:00)
Spotty glasses. Crusty faucets. Soap that won't lather. If you have a water softener, those are signs it needs a little attention. The good news: most water softener maintenance takes a few minutes and costs almost nothing. Let's look inside one, and then go through the six jobs that keep it working.

## 1. How a water softener works (0:25)
A typical softener has two tanks. The tall one is the resin tank. It's filled with tiny plastic beads called resin. Hard water flows in at the top, and as it passes through, the beads grab the calcium and magnesium that make water hard. Soft water comes out the bottom and goes to your faucets.
But the beads can only hold so much. So every few days, the softener regenerates. It pulls salty water, called brine, from the second tank, the brine tank. That brine washes the hardness off the beads and down the drain, and the beads are ready to go again.
So the whole system depends on three things: enough salt, brine that can actually flow, and clean resin. Every job in this video protects one of those.

## 2. Check the salt level monthly (1:35)
Job one. Once a month, lift the brine tank lid. Keep the tank at least one-third to half full of salt, and keep the salt above the water level. Many tanks have a fill line inside.
If you can see standing water above the salt, it's time to add more.

## 3. Use the right salt (2:00)
Job two: use salt made for water softeners. Pellets or crystals both work. Avoid rock salt. It has more insoluble material, and that builds up as sludge in the bottom of the tank. And try to stick to one type, because mixing them can encourage bridging. Which brings us to job three.

## 4. Break up salt bridges (2:25)
A salt bridge is a hard crust that forms above the water. From the top, the tank looks full. But underneath there's an empty gap, so the salt never reaches the water, no brine is made, and your water turns hard again.
To check, push a broom handle down through the salt in a few spots. If it stops on a hard layer with empty space below, gently break it up. Don't hit the tank walls. When it's right, the handle slides all the way to the bottom.

## 5. Clean the brine tank once a year (3:10)
Job four, once a year. Put the softener in bypass mode. Let the salt run low, then scoop or vacuum out what's left, salt and water. Scrub the tank with mild soap and water, and rinse. Add a few inches of water and fresh salt, as your manual says, and take the softener out of bypass.

## 6. Clean the resin with resin cleaner (3:45)
Job five protects the beads. Over time, iron and other minerals coat the resin, and it can't grab hardness as well. A resin cleaner strips that off. Use it every three to twelve months, more often if your water has iron or you see rust stains.
Pour the dose into the brine well or tank as the label says, then start a manual regeneration so the cleaner rinses through the resin. Test your water the next day.

## 7. Check the injector and screen (4:25)
Job six. If the brine tank stays full of water after a regeneration, the softener isn't drawing brine. The usual cause is the injector, also called the venturi: a tiny nozzle that sucks brine out of the tank. It's easy to clog.
Put the softener in bypass and release the pressure, as your manual shows. Remove the injector cap, and rinse the screen and nozzle in warm water. Put a little silicone grease on the O-rings, and reassemble. After the next regeneration, the water level in the brine tank should drop.

## 8. Test your water (5:15)
Finally, test. A day after the softener regenerates, use hardness test strips on water from a cold kitchen faucet. Soft water, and no new spots on glasses or fixtures, means you're done.

## 9. When to call a pro (5:35)
Call a water treatment technician if the water stays hard even with plenty of salt and no bridge, if the brine tank stays full of water, if the control valve leaks, or if the softener runs constantly.

## 10. Outro (5:50)
That's it: check the salt, use the right salt, break bridges, clean the tank, clean the resin, and keep the injector clear. The full step-by-step guide, with a checklist, is on homeimprovementhub101.com. Thanks for watching.
